01 / 05 · Why rhythm
01 · The signal is already there

~120 passwords per person, typed dozens of times a day.

Every login already carries dwell (how long a key is held) and flight (the gap to the next key). CypherKey reads timing that is being produced anyway.

Timing captured via performance.now() · sub-millisecond
02 · What is at risk

39% of breaches start with a stolen credential.

A leaked password works from anywhere, for anyone. The dark-web email says the secret is out. Nothing in a password alone can tell who is typing it.

Credential abuse share of breaches, industry reporting
03 · Why biometric rhythm

A second factor you cannot leave at home.

Rhythm is a behavioral biometric you carry in your hands. No phone to grab, no code to copy, no key to lose or charge. It works with the passwords you already have.

Feature vector 3n+5 · flight weighted 1.5×
04 · Easy and safe

Zero-knowledge vault. Visible light whenever it listens.

The server holds only ciphertext and an Argon2id hash. Raw keystrokes are destroyed after each sample; only means and floored variances remain. Open source, self-host in one command.

MIT client · AGPL server · σ floor 8 ms

Origin · 2007 → 2026

Written in 2007 to tell a human from a bot. It told people apart instead.

Keystroke Captcha was a telemetry script inside a custom MVC framework, listening to key events to decide whether a form was being filled in by a person. It measured dwell and flight — and the timings did not just separate humans from machines, they separated one person from another.
